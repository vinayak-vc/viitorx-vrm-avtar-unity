# S-01 - Real-time human cutout: the person in a camera picture, isolated from the background, shown in Unity (2026-10-13)

Decision: **ADR-103**. Tasks: `tasks.md` S-01. Sidecar repo: `docs/tasks.md` S-01. Showcase: `Showcase/Cutout/README.md`. Follows H-04.

The request: capture a person through a live RGB camera, separate them from the background with AI, and show the isolated person in Unity; **Python perceives, Unity renders**; modes Original Camera, Person CutOut (transparent or a chosen background) and Colored Silhouette (red among them), switchable without restarting anything; independent of the VRM and of skeleton tracking; the picture and the mask reusable by other Unity systems; limitations stated plainly. Reference clips in `Ref/` (a true-colour cutout with a glowing outline, red silhouettes, particle textures, echo copies) show where it is going; **those effects are NOT part of this milestone** (s.9).

## 1. How it works

```
camera / LAN camera / stream / file
        |  (Python sidecar, tools/cutout/cutout_udp_sender.py)
   person detector (YOLOX-s) -> tracker (stable ids, held through dropouts)
        |  one SQUARE CROP per person; every other person's box painted out of it
   MediaPipe selfie segmenter (256x256 ONNX, DirectML)  ->  probability map per crop
        |  max-merge into one canvas -> matte: guided filter, temporal smoothing, edge ramp, cleanup
   uint8 mask  +  the picture (raw RGBA, or JPEG)
        |  UDP 127.0.0.1:8903, a frame cut into 60 000-byte datagrams (a 72-byte header on each)
Unity (VirtualMirror.Vision, VisionHost)
   CutoutReceiver (thread) -> CutoutAssembler -> newest whole frame, handed over by swapping buffers
   CutoutTextures: picture (RGBA32 or a flipped JPEG) + mask (R8) as GPU textures   <- reusable by anything
   CutoutView: two quads + the VirtualMirror/Cutout shader: Original / Person / Silhouette, glow, mirror, backdrop
```

**Unity runs no model and no segmentation.** It receives the picture and its mask and chooses how to show them. Every display switch is a shader parameter: nothing restarts, the camera is never reopened, the sender is told nothing.

Why a crop per person: the selfie network sees 256 pixels across what it is given, so a dancer a quarter of a 1080p frame tall reaches it 65 pixels tall, and two of them come back as one blob with the curtain and the floor lights attached. A square crop around each detected person puts each at ~256 px. Why the other people are painted out of each crop (`isolate`): a tall box makes a wide square, so the neighbour is inside it, and the network, asked about a pair, joins them with whatever dark background lies between (s.4).

## 3. The wire in one paragraph

A NEW message on UDP **8903** (hands 8901, face 8902 and body 8899 untouched), binary because a picture does not survive being text. Every datagram starts with the same 72-byte header (session, frame number, chunk index / count / offset, sizes, encodings, flags, model id, people, the sender's own latency figures), so any one datagram says which frame it belongs to and where it goes. Colour: raw RGBA32, JPEG, or none (mask only). Mask: raw A8 or raw deflate. Top row first. A newer frame abandons an unfinished older one, an older chunk is ignored, a repeated chunk is ignored, a new session id is a new producer. The documented layout is `cutout_wire.py`; it is pinned to the C# reader by 26 golden datagrams written by the production encoder (`Tests/EditMode/VisionFixtures/cutout_*.bin`, drift-checked by `tests/test_vision_fixtures.py`).

## 6. The limits, stated plainly

The model is a ~0.5 MB network trained for selfies and video calls, run once per person crop. It is reliable on a lone standing or sitting person in an ordinary room and it is **not** reliable on everything:

- **Dark clothes on a dark background.** The network labels dark curtain next to dark trousers as person (a "cape" behind a dancer's raised arm; the legs of the dark-trousered dancer partly see-through at times). Isolation removes the blob that used to join two people standing apart; it does not remove it when they touch or overlap (their boxes overlap, and overlapping people cannot be told apart by their boxes).
- **Thin gaps** between an arm and the torso are filled, so a pale wall shows as a halo between them (visible on `video.webm`).
- **Bright floor lights and reflections** next to the feet are sometimes kept as part of the person (the red and blue stage lights on the two-person clip).
- **Low-confidence patches** of the network are blocky; the guided filter and the temporal filter hide most, not all. An outline is soft by design (an edge ramp), and flickers by ~2 % of the foreground per frame pair on the measured clips.
- **Far people.** With no person detector the whole picture goes through one 256-pixel network: fine for a face filling a webcam, poor for someone far away. The detector is `models/yolox_s.onnx` (fetch with `tools/model/fetch_yolox_s.py`); without it the sender says so and falls back.
- **The people count** is the larger of the people tracked and the foreground groups; a false detection (a statue) counts.
- **One camera, one reader.** The cutout sender and the hands or face sender cannot read the same webcam at once; each stream is `Auto` per scene.
- **A built player has no sender**: `tools/` is not shipped by `SidecarBuildPostprocessor` (root `*.py` only) and the new modules are at the sidecar root only for the Editor and the Python repo. Running the cutout from a build needs the sender started by hand.
- **Throughput** here is 18-30 fps depending on the clip and on the GPU's clock; the Editor itself takes ~25 % of the frames at 1080p with the IMGUI HUD.
- **JPEG pictures** are decoded on Unity's main thread, and a **deflated mask** is inflated on the receive thread with a small allocation per frame (`DeflateStream`): both are network options, the default (raw, raw) allocates nothing per frame.

## 7. NOT TESTED (and designed only)

- **A person on the webcam.** The only webcam run found nobody (s.4). Quality on a live person is judged from the two video files, not from a camera. Run `python-sidecar~\scripts\run_cutout_demo.bat` and stand in view.
- **A phone / LAN camera, rtsp and http streams** through this sender (the source code is shared with the hands and face senders, which have not run on one either).
- **A network between two machines**, `--chunk-bytes 1200`, JPEG and deflate through a real sender into Unity (they are tested with golden datagrams and synthetic frames).
- **A built player**, the Launcher row on screen (the catalogue is unit-tested; the scene is registered in Build Settings on this machine only: that file is gitignored), several people beyond two, a long soak (memory over hours), a second display, a non-16:9 window beyond the unit-tested placement maths.
- **Designed, not built**: particle / voxel silhouettes, the camera-to-silhouette transition, disperse and reform, motion echo (fading copies), edge trails. They need only `VisionHost.CutoutTextures` (the picture, the mask, `Version`, the foreground fraction) and `CutoutView`'s quad: a VFX Graph or compute pass samples the two textures and never segments anything. The glow outline and the colour treatments are built, as a shader.

## 8. Traps found on the way

- Unity decodes a JPEG upright (top row at v = 1) and the wire sends the raw rows top first: without a flip the JPEG picture would have been upside down relative to its own mask. `CutoutTextures` blits the JPEG into a render texture with a negative scale so both textures have row 0 at the top.
- `UdpVisionReceiver<T>` had the socket loop inside it; the cutout needed another datagram shape and 16 MB of receive queue (a 720p frame is a burst of ~66 datagrams of 60 KB). The loop moved to `UdpDatagramReceiver`; the default 1 MB queue would have dropped most of every frame.
- A test that exercises a rule must not be satisfiable by another rule: the payload-cap test passed with the cap deleted because the byte-sum rule refused the same frame. Found by the mutation check.
- The matte's "people" is a count of foreground GROUPS joined by proximity: two dancers a metre apart read as 1 on 808 of 894 frames while both were tracked on every frame. The engine now reports the larger of the groups and the people tracked.
- A background model (the median of the clip, an oracle) did not help: the network was confident about the curtain blobs, and the veto cut the dark trousers instead. A box prior (mask limited to each person's box grown 10-25 %) did not help either: the blobs lay inside the boxes. Both tried, measured on contact sheets, dropped.
- Setting a SerializedObject on the open scene before Play survives Play; the scene is rebuilt from `CutoutSceneBuilder` afterwards (never saved with a test video in it).
- `execute_code` callbacks for timed screenshots must kill themselves when Play ends.

## 9. Extending it (designed)

`VisionHost.CutoutTextures` is one object for the life of the host: `ColorTexture`, `MaskTexture` (top row first: sample at `(u, 1 - v)` or use `CutoutTextures.UvScale/UvOffset`), `Version` (changes every upload), `Generation` (changes when a texture is recreated), the sender's facts. `host.CutoutUpdated` fires once per uploaded frame. A system that wants the person (an emitter that spawns from mask pixels, an echo ring buffer of render textures, a dissolve driven by `1 - mask`) binds a `CutoutStreamBinder` (no view) to ask for the stream and reads those two textures. The mask has soft edges and covers several people at once; a per-person mask or an instance id is NOT in the wire (the sender could add it: a second mask section, a new `kind`; an ADR in both repos).
